Summary

This analysis evaluated a targeted small RNA sequencing dataset consisting of three Illumina NextSeq-derived BAM files. The assay showed technically successful targeted enrichment, with high-confidence alignments, reproducible read-length structure, and recurrent enrichment across cancer-associated loci. Molecule-aware barcode deduplication revealed that raw read depth was partially inflated by amplification redundancy, particularly in sample3. Candidate variants were identified within enriched genes, but are interpreted as exploratory RNA-derived observations requiring orthogonal validation. Soft-clipped read analysis suggested a structured mixture of repetitive sequence mapping, homologous alignment ambiguity, off-target enrichment, and complex RNA fragment behavior.

Study Objective

The goal was to evaluate sequencing and alignment quality, identify enriched genes and targeted loci, characterize candidate nucleotide variants, and investigate the origin and interpretation of large soft-clipped read segments. Because the assay is targeted small RNA sequencing rather than whole-transcriptome or DNA sequencing, all findings are interpreted in the context of targeted enrichment, short-fragment alignment behavior, and molecule-aware barcode deduplication.

Analysis Workflow

The workflow was organized to separate assay QC, molecule-aware enrichment, exploratory variant calling, and soft-clip interpretation.

1

BAM Inspection

Reviewed alignment structure, read lengths, mapping quality, secondary alignments, and molecular barcode tags.

2

QC Profiling

Summarized read-length distributions, MAPQ behavior, barcode complexity, and amplification redundancy.

3

Gene Enrichment

Aggregated reads into recurrent regions and annotated enriched loci with GENCODE v19.

4

Variant Analysis

Used bcftools-based candidate calling within enriched target regions, interpreted cautiously as RNA-derived evidence.

5

Soft-Clip Analysis

Extracted large soft-clipped segments and remapped them to assess repetitive, ambiguous, or structurally complex origins.

Quality Control Overview

Alignment-level QC showed strong targeted assay behavior. Samples shared reproducible fragment-length peaks around approximately 130 bp and 150 bp, and most reads aligned with high confidence. However, sample3 showed reduced molecular complexity and elevated reads-per-barcode ratios, suggesting increased amplification redundancy relative to samples 1 and 2.

Candidate Variant Analysis

Candidate nucleotide substitutions and small indels were evaluated within enriched target regions using a bcftools-based strategy. Multiple candidate variants were identified in cancer-associated genes including FGFR1, AXL, RAF1, EGFR, MET, CCND1, and RAB7A. FGFR1 showed recurrent high-confidence substitutions across all three samples, while sample2 showed the broadest overall candidate variant burden.

Soft-Clipped Read Analysis

Large soft-clipped reads were investigated to assess whether they reflected artifacts, repetitive sequence mapping, alignment ambiguity, or structurally complex RNA fragments. Samples 1 and 2 showed more constrained soft-clipping distributions, while sample3 had broader clipping behavior extending beyond 100 bp. Soft clips were recurrent at specific loci, including a strong signal near the enriched CCND1 region on chromosome 11.

Discussion

The most important interpretation is that this dataset behaves like a targeted sequencing assay with coherent enrichment at cancer-associated loci, rather than a random or failed sequencing experiment. The read-length, MAPQ, and enrichment profiles collectively support a technically successful assay. However, molecule-aware analysis shows that read depth and molecular abundance diverge in sample3, making barcode-aware deduplication necessary for any quantitative comparison.

The variant and soft-clipping analyses are best viewed as hypothesis-generating. Candidate variants localized to biologically relevant genes, but targeted RNA sequencing cannot distinguish somatic DNA variation from RNA editing, allele-specific expression, reverse-transcription artifacts, or amplification-related effects without additional validation. Similarly, recurrent soft-clipped segments show meaningful structure, but remapping supports a mixed origin involving repetitive reference content and alignment ambiguity rather than a single dominant fusion event.

Conclusion

Collectively, the dataset represents a technically successful targeted small RNA-seq assay with biologically structured enrichment patterns. The strongest findings are the recurrent enrichment of cancer-associated loci and the clear value of molecule-aware barcode deduplication, particularly for sample3. Candidate variants and soft-clipped reads provide useful exploratory signals, but downstream biological or clinical interpretation would require orthogonal validation and careful accounting for amplification redundancy and repetitive-sequence alignment behavior.
